VERDICT. GPR88 is detectable only in jawed vertebrates: human 790.9, mouse
792.0, zebrafish 528.8. Every other lineage, lamprey (62.1) and hagfish (63.9)
included, falls in the 31-128 class-A background, 4x below zebrafish. There is
no intermediate score anywhere, so this is not a gradient being cut by a
threshold - it is a gap.

  C. elegans: best 53.1 (Q22188), below even the porous floor. BLAST agrees
  independently at 53.9 bits / 24.9% id. No GPR88 ortholog in C. elegans.

LIMIT, and it is a real one. Three jawed-vertebrate sequences make this the
most narrowly anchored profile in the project - more so than the melatonin
one, which had eight to ten. A sufficiently divergent invertebrate GPR88
ortholog would be invisible to it, so the invertebrate negative is
method-limited, NOT a confirmed absence. Treat it as weaker than the
C. elegans melatonin negative, which had two orthogonal axes and a tree.

One lead if that is worth chasing: the top invertebrate hits are Drosophila
Moody (68.3) and an amphioxus "GPCR moody-like" (83.9), and the earlier BLAST
also returned Moody first in the fly (57.4). Two methods pointing at the same
family at twilight-zone scores is weak but consistent, and moody is where an
invertebrate relative of GPR88 would be looked for. Not tested here.

AND: GPR88 is not related to the family PCDR-1 is in, tested both ways.
GPR52 and GPR21 score BELOW THRESHOLD against this profile, and GPR88 scores
14.5 against the orphan7 family profile (rank 691 of 707 in human). The two
families are mutually undetectable - a symmetric negative.
